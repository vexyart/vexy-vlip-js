    How playback works¶

    This page explains what the player does between loading a track and showing a card. It follows the five-cue sample.vtt used by the demos: cards start at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 seconds.

    Source layout¶

    File Role
    src/index.js Public ESM entry. Re-exports the class and helpers; registers nothing.
    src/player.js The VexyVlip class: DOM, loading, both playback modes, eased travel, Start/Replay screen, events.
    src/vtt.js Pure WebVTT parsing and cue-to-card conversion. No DOM.
    src/segments.js Pure stop-point navigation: nextStop, prevStop, currentStop, segmentAtTime.
    src/cards.js Builds card elements and applies placement and inline styles.
    src/styles.js The stylesheet as a string, for the shadow root or one <head> injection.
    src/element.js The <vexy-vlip> custom element.
    src/global.js Entry for the classic-script IIFE build.

    Loading¶

    1. The constructor merges your options over DEFAULTS, creates the <video>, the card layer, the controls and the Start/Replay screen, and attaches listeners.
    2. If vtt is empty and track is set, the player downloads the track with fetch(). A failed request emits vexyvlip:error with phase: "track".
    3. The text goes through parseCards(), which produces one card model per cue.
    4. When both the video metadata and the cards are in, the player is ready. It applies startSegment or startAt, emits vexyvlip:ready, and either starts playback (continuous mode with autoplay), opens on the start segment, or shows the Start screen over the dimmed first frame.

    The frame loop¶

    While the video plays, the player checks the playhead once per frame. It uses video.requestVideoFrameCallback() where the browser supports it, and requestAnimationFrame() otherwise. Only one callback is queued at a time.

    Continuous mode¶

    On each frame the player calls segmentAtTime(cards, currentTime). When the result changes, it shows the new card (segmententer, cardshow) or hides the old one (cardhide). Playback is never paused for a card.

    With sample.vtt, the card for cue 1 is visible from 2.0 s to 3.5 s, then no card is visible until 4.0 s.

    Stepped mode¶

    Stepped mode advances by card index, not by time, so a cue at 00:00.000 and back-to-back cues each get their own stop.

    1. The viewer advances (click, Space, Next, or next()). The current card hides and the player emits segmentexit and cardhide.
    2. The target is the start of the next card. With sample.vtt, resting on card 0 (2 s) gives a target of 4 s.
    3. With easing: "linear" the video plays natively. On each frame the player checks currentTime + 0.02 >= target, stopping slightly early to avoid overshoot.
    4. At the stop the player pauses, snaps currentTime to exactly the cue start, shows the card, and emits segmententer, cardshow and stop.
    5. After the last card, the next advance plays to the end of the video. The player emits ended and shows the Replay screen.

    prev() goes back by one index and shows that card immediately, with no playback.

    Eased travel¶

    When easing is ease-in, ease-out or ease-in-out, stepped advances do not use native playback:

    1. The video pauses and is muted, because scrubbed audio is unusable.

    2. A requestAnimationFrame loop sets currentTime from the current stop to the target. The trip takes (target − start) / playbackRate seconds of wall time, and progress is mapped through the curve:

      Easing Curve f(p)
      linear p
      ease-in p²
      ease-out 1 − (1 − p)²
      ease-in-out p² (3 − 2p) (smoothstep)

    3. At the target the original mute state is restored and the stop is handled as in step 4 above.

    Eased travel depends on fast seeking. Encode the video with fast-start and a short GOP; see Installation.

    Card placement¶

    A card model's placement holds x, y, an optional w, an anchor and an align. The card element is positioned at (x, y) inside the video box and shifted with a CSS transform so the chosen anchor point of the card lands on that position. Numbers are percentages; strings are used as CSS lengths.

    Why not the native caption renderer¶

    The browser's ::cue pseudo-element cannot set arbitrary backgrounds, padding, radius, shadows or exact placement, and its cues are not regular DOM. Vexy Vlip renders each cue as an ordinary element instead: the card layer is an aria-live="polite" region and each card has role="note", so screen readers announce new cards.

    Scope¶

    The project's specification limits it to one sentence: play one video, drive styled HTML cards from its WebVTT track, and let the viewer step through the video card by card or watch it continuously, using native browser APIs only. Transcoding, adaptive streaming, analytics and editing are out of scope.
